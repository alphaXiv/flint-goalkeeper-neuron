# Autonomous Flint goalkeeper-direction study

## Result

Researcher Flint selected the positive neuron-pole `L20E73N184P` after scanning a separately loaded Subject Flint. Its strongest natural activation contexts were dominated by field-hockey goalkeeper passages.

At factor 64, two Flint-authored confirmations produced `goalkeeper` in 7/8 and 8/8 target continuations. Baseline, opposite-pole, and norm-matched-neuron conditions produced zero matches across 48 control continuations.

A subsequent preregistered follow-up froze the candidate, factor, regex, decoding setup, and controls while replacing the sports prompts with 16 unrelated, operator-authored requests. It produced 16/16 target matches and zero matches across 48 controls.

## Method

The subject was `RecursiveSI/flint`. A separate hosted Flint acted as researcher through an agent loop with file tools and a stable lab API. The operator did not supply the successful coordinate, interpretation, dose, confirmation prompts, or regex during the autonomous discovery run.

The intervention added the selected signed neuron output direction to the routed MLP output during generation. It bypassed natural expert routing. Every confirmation compared the target direction against no intervention, the opposite pole, and a same-layer scanned neuron with similar write-vector norm.

## Interpretation

Natural activation evidence and causal intervention evidence agree on a goalkeeper association. The out-of-domain follow-up shows that the observed text injection is not restricted to sports-adjacent prompts.

The study does not establish a perfectly monosemantic neuron during ordinary routed inference. The factor is large, routing is bypassed, the endpoint is a literal whole-output match, and several generations are repetitive or malformed.
